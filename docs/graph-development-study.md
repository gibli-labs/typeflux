# Matched graph development pilot

Issue [#86](https://github.com/gibli-labs/typeflux/issues/86) evaluates ordinary
repository search, graph retrieval and graph-assisted source assembly. The
`tools.development.study86` harness runs a localized, one-shot patch experiment;
it does not measure full interactive-agent productivity or human PR acceptance.

## Observed result, 2026-11-06

This pilot did **not** meet the predeclared adoption threshold. Retain the existing
advisory graph and keep source assembly experimental. It does not establish a
productivity gain, safe selective CI, general model quality or broader autonomy.

The [curated observations](verification/graph-study-matched-2026-11-06.json)
retain the frozen protocol, source/CLI/image hashes, task revisions, inert candidate
patches/text, actual native case outcomes, grader results and the reconciled cost
ledger. Both protocol freezes are linked below. All 54 planned slot outcomes and
six robustness outcomes were recorded. There were 43 primary model invocations;
11 successor slots were blocked without another call. “Complete” means every
planned slot has an outcome, not that every slot ran or passed product tests.

| Context arm | Accepted / 18 planned slots | Accepted by repeat | Allocated CLI estimate |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Fixed ordinary search | 0 / 18 | 0, 0 | $4.016021 |
| Graph retrieval | 0 / 18 | 0, 0 | $3.395472 |
| Graph source assembly | 2 / 18 | 2, 0 | $2.892800 |

The two accepted assembly outcomes were the dependent-PR document rubric and the
console read's four native query-engine cases, both in the first repeat. Neither
success repeated. The arm estimates include failed primary invocations and their
document grades; shared calibration, robustness and the earlier resource pilot
are separate. Cost per accepted patch is undefined for the zero-acceptance arms;
it cannot support a comparative efficiency claim.

Of the 54 slots, 32 failed the diff/output contract, three failed the document
rubric, six failed native checks and 11 were blocked. Seven candidates reached
native validation: 105 case executions, 74 passed and 31 failed, with no skipped
cases. Repeated case identities are not independent observations. One follow-up
ran against its own applicable but previously failing graph-CI predecessor and
failed 15 of 38 cases; the other 11 successors were blocked. This measures failed
end-to-end sequences, not general architectural degradation or conditional quality
of unattempted changes.

Read-only post-run Git diagnostics found malformed hunk/header/output contracts
for all 32 primary format rejections and all six robustness probes. They did not
repair patches or relabel outcomes. The format bottleneck limits conclusions
about functional correctness and graph utility; absent native consumer outcomes
are unassessed. No accepted robustness probe is not a proof of injection safety.
The separate 58 parameterized deterministic guard cases (36 registered test
functions) passed, and the 14 offline
baseline/reference controls discriminated with owned cleanup before the amendment
froze. These are distinct evidence classes.

Total study estimate was **$15.220329 across 66 CLI invocations**, including the
failed initial resource pilot, both sets of six calibrations, primary calls,
document grades and robustness probes. There are no pending paid outcomes. This
is the CLI estimate, not a provider invoice. Ordinary Fable code reviews and Codex
orchestration are outside the study ledger. Real post-response interruption
reconciled without another model call in both protocols. No native infrastructure
fault occurred during the matched run; controlled offline tests cover that path.

Complete paired task/repeat differences were 0 for retrieval and 2/18 for assembly.
Assembly's leave-one-family-out range was 0.0625–0.142857; this is descriptive
sensitivity, not a confidence interval. No family won both repeats, and the
baseline cost ratio was undefined. These results do not meet the adoption rule.

### Evidence limits

Existing starting-revision regression tests may appear in context. New reference
test additions and solution patches were withheld; the validation groups combine
those new requirements with existing regressions, so not every case is unseen.
This is not the original full interactive-agent baseline, and human acceptance,
review labor, maintenance labor and unobserved regressions remain unmeasured.
The smaller, resource-informed sample and two correlated repeats cannot establish
population speedup, slowdown or a general lack of graph benefit.

The [v1 source reconstruction patch](verification/graph-study-v1-source.patch)
was applied only in a private temporary copy and matched all 39 original source
hashes. It preserves the failed pilot for inspection; never apply it to the working
repository, rerun paid calls or reset the cumulative ledger to reproduce a charge.

## Protocol

The [original freeze](https://github.com/gibli-labs/typeflux/issues/86#issuecomment-6683030577)
and [resource amendment](https://github.com/gibli-labs/typeflux/issues/86#issuecomment-6683587658)
were recorded before their respective paid calls.

The [retained resource pilot](verification/graph-study-resource-pilot-2026-11-06.json)
had six passing grader calibrations and one failed first coding invocation. It
reported $1.4499 for that invocation, 6,144 output tokens despite requested 2,048,
a CLI max-budget error and no patch. Total pilot estimate was $2.35516. Its real
post-response interruption reconciled without another model call. No candidate
held-out validation had run when it stopped. The original 12-slot/72-cell protocol
and failure remain in that record; they are not a completed matched study.

The amendment raises the per-response allowance, lowers primary context and sets
a $2 CLI budget with at least $3 next-call allowance uniformly and reduces the sample
to nine slots/54 cells. It excludes provider-defaults, neutral-manifest and cleanup
implementation tasks after the resource pilot using preflight scope/cost information, before observing
any candidate held-out performance. The failed CI-diagnostics task is retained. This favors smaller components and further limits
coverage. All earlier pilot costs remain in the same cumulative ledger. Requested
token and CLI budget settings are not proven hard limits on an entire invocation;
post-call estimates and the new-call stop/reserve remain authoritative.

The authorized study ceiling is $50. Every trial, documentation grade, calibration
and robustness call shares one journal ledger. Claude CLI reports cost estimates,
not provider invoices. New calls stop when the cumulative estimate plus a $3
next-call allowance (raised automatically to any larger observed invocation estimate) would exceed $40; the remaining $10 is headroom for
client overruns. Unknown paid outcomes stop execution until reconciled. Normal
Fable implementation reviews and unmetered Codex orchestration are separate.
Completing all 78 possible calls below $40 requires an average estimate below
about $0.44 per invocation after the $2.35516 carryover and $3 next-call allowance; all six calibrations rerun under the amended settings. The amended CLI budget is $2;
$3 allows more than the observed 1.45x overrun. At the first call's observed aggregate
input, 8,192 output tokens would estimate about $1.56 at the installed CLI rates;
that sizing is not a guaranteed cap on an unseen call.
An unknown charge with no authoritative response remains unresolved and stops the
study. Never invent a cost, edit the journal, reset the budget or change request
identity to bypass that stop; report unreached cells if it cannot be reconciled.

The fixed model is `claude-fable-5`, low effort, no tools, no MCP, no automatic paid-trial
repair; network retries requested zero, at most 8,192 requested output tokens and 32,000 primary context characters. Freeze
records the installed CLI version/binary hash, all harness/guard source hashes,
immutable Docker image ID, task revisions, held-out inputs, order and thresholds.
Freeze also records installed help/option-parser evidence without requesting a
completion. Every paid response checks the observed usage schema after recording
its known estimate and before any further calls. Environment limits are requested
CLI settings, not independently attested provider guarantees.
Preflight uses closed stdin, a 15-second timeout and dummy authentication pointed
at loopback port zero; it cannot fall through to the provider endpoint. Paid calls
use the already logged-in CLI/keychain account; environment-only API keys are
intentionally not forwarded. The CLI runs serially in empty, opaque directories without repository settings,
skills or history. Each arm has the same system instructions and resource limits.

The sample is reduced from the proposed 30–50 independent tasks to nine slots:
seven historical tasks and two follow-ups against each arm's own earlier patch.
There are two repeats and three arms, at most 54 primary calls, twelve document
grades, six grader calibrations and six graph robustness probes. Historical
Python/TypeScript engine changes share a family; development-tool tasks also
correlate. This is an affordable descriptive pilot with limited coverage, not a
representative SDK/contract/deployment population study.

Historical source and graphs come from each real starting revision. Retrospective
component filenames localize each assignment; solution patches and newly added reference test
code are absent from primary packets. Source windows and fixed search terms are
bounded; a missing graph mapping falls back to ordinary search. Some graph and
assembly packets can be identical. Snapshot Git objects contain only the starting
commit and its reachable past. Successors receive their own predecessor changes,
including applicable predecessors that failed held-outs; inapplicable predecessors
block a successor instead of silently substituting the reference solution.

Validation uses an offline, non-root, read-only Docker container: two CPUs, 2 GiB
RAM, 256 PIDs, a 512 MiB temporary filesystem and a 180-second container wait.
Actual Docker inspect values must match the CPU/memory/PID/network/user limits
before starting candidate code. Source, validators and dependencies are distinct mounts. Only frozen third-party
dependencies remain in the image; historical TypeScript SDK source is rebuilt per
candidate. Component checks are not the full mandatory suite or tamper-resistant
attestation of arbitrary adversarial code. Exact case counts, exits, skips, errors,
OOM status and owned cleanup must all pass. No credentials or Docker socket are
mounted into candidates.

Six historical code baseline/reference pairs must discriminate before freezing. An
additional prospective whitespace-registry control must fail three new cases on
the historical fix and pass all 38 after its small reference extension. Document
rubrics use a separate, arm-blinded prompt to the same model, not independent
weights or human review; reference, empty and unsafe calibration controls precede
trials. Failed calibration stops calls. Invalid patches or grades receive no repair
attempt. A deliberate coordinator interruption after the first saved response
exercises resume without resubmitting the paid request. Stale/deleted/injected
graph context and deterministic policy-weakening refusals are recorded separately.

## Reproduction

After the maintained setup in [development.md](development.md), create a Docker
build context with `git archive a2143eac495f60a59843e18aaef7d898abba231c` extracted
under `repository/`. Copy `verifier.Dockerfile` as `Dockerfile` and `prune_image.py`
from `tools/development/study86/`. Build using the `desktop-linux` context and save
the resulting image ID. Base digests are pinned; Debian package resolution is not
fully reproducible, so retain the actual image ID and its
`/environment/debian-packages.txt` inventory rather than claiming identical rebuilds.

Keep pack caches and output directories on the same filesystem for immutable
Git-object hardlinks. Use fresh absolute output directories; never overwrite old attempts:

```sh
uv run --project packages/python python -m tools.development.study86.controls \
  --image sha256:ACTUAL_IMAGE_ID --output /private/tmp/study86-controls
# Run test_study86_*.py with tools.development.pytest_results. Retain its exact
# native report, actual exit code and matching before/after harness sourceHashes.
uv run --project packages/python python -m tools.development.study86 freeze \
  --image sha256:ACTUAL_IMAGE_ID --output /private/tmp/study86-run \
  --controls /private/tmp/study86-controls/results.json \
  --successor-controls /private/tmp/study86-controls/successor-results.json \
  --guard-report /private/tmp/study86-guards.json
# Review and record protocol.json before this next command starts paid calls.
uv run --project packages/python python -m tools.development.study86 run \
  --output /private/tmp/study86-run --exercise-after-response-interruption
# After exit 130, inspect the saved response/pending effect, then resume the same
# command and frozen directory. Never remove its journal or resubmit the request.
uv run --project packages/python python -m tools.development.study86 analyze \
  --output /private/tmp/study86-run
```

Each Docker attempt uses the existing deployed-review resource owner/ID helper
with a unique owner and ledger; it shares no deployment resource. A failed cleanup
or unfinished prior resource requires ownership reconciliation before validation
can resume. Genuine Docker faults stop new calls without finalizing a trial;
resuming revalidates the same saved paid response for free after owned effects are
reconciled. A container wait timeout with a responding daemon records a candidate
time-budget failure and owned cleanup, rather than retrying the candidate. This
is a failure under the configured budget, not proof of its underlying cause.
Preserve failures, raw responses and estimates privately. Public
results must omit machine paths and raw diagnostic payloads.

## Interpretation

CLI result turn counts are recorded separately from API response counts, which
are not exposed here. Aggregate output above the requested per-response cap is
flagged as an overrun/possible continuation, not a measured response count. An
inapplicable predecessor blocks its successor without spending another call;
blocked cells are outcomes, not passes. They limit interpretation and cannot
be treated as an independent positive quality result.

Report held-out acceptance, regressions within those checks, implementation versus
infrastructure failures, token/model/cost estimates, context/model/validation/review
time, known consumer failures, successive quality and failed/unreached cells.
Outside-assigned-scope source characters are only a context proxy; dependencies
can be useful. Declaration edits are zero by fixed scope, not zero human graph
maintenance. Human labor, provider invoices and unobserved regressions are not
measured. Automated grading/correction counts do not replace human review effort.

Use complete paired task/repeat comparisons and leave-one-family-out sensitivity;
these ranges are not confidence intervals. Assembly is a positive pilot signal
only if it wins at least two complete families in both repeats, passes the declared
policy guards and costs at most 1.25 times search per accepted patch. Even a
positive result keeps assembly experimental and requires independent validation
before broader use. No selective CI is proposed, so no selected-suite equivalence
is claimed. Full mandatory CI, reviews and exact-main release controls remain.

This is development evaluation data, not runtime metadata. It uses no metadata
contributors and changes no workflow execution manifest.
